# Some practical ways to get started

Lesson 54 min

In this lessonBy the end, you’ll be able to

* Apply the four principles of healthy human-agent teams: clear roles, a written north star, gradual release, and the right information access
* Explain how work changes when your team moves from single-player AI to multiplayer AI

A short checklist for your first two weeks, with links to go deeper. Start soon, but start small.

## Getting started checklist[](#getting-started-checklist)

1. **Pick one collaboration space, three to five people, and one agent.**
   Start with a small team that has a project to complete together. Teams with high trust work best for initial experimentation.
2. **Configure a multiplayer agent.**
   There are lots of ways to do this, the most common being [Claude Tag(opens in new tab)](https://claude.com/docs/claude-tag/users/getting-started) ↗ or [the Claude Managed Agents API(opens in new tab)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/quickstart) ↗.
3. **Make all the ways the team works open and shared by default.**
   If a decision happens in a meeting or a DM, someone posts it the same day. For an agent, if it isn't written down and accessible, it doesn't exist. [Why working in public matters(opens in new tab)](https://claude.com/blog/building-effective-human-agent-teams) ↗
4. **Write the roster, agents included.**
   List every recurring job and give each job one owner. Ask the agent to propose the list from what it can read, then let people decide. [Defined roles with the right tools(opens in new tab)](https://claude.com/blog/building-effective-human-agent-teams) ↗
5. **Write down the north star and pin it where the agent can read it.**
   A north star is an ambitious goal that's measurable. People set the north star and agents help work towards it.
6. **Give the agent one visible job first.**
   A morning briefing shared to the entire team is a great task to start with. For the first few days, a person should review the briefing and give the agent feedback on its effectiveness. [How Slack's teams started(opens in new tab)](https://claude.com/blog/turning-conversation-into-knowledge-how-slack-builds-human-agent-teams) ↗
7. **Build checks that agents can use.**
   For every task you ask an agent to do, give it a rubric for what "passing" looks like. This scales trust the most quickly between humans and agents.
8. **Extend trust a step at a time, per kind of task.**
   After the agent has handled one kind of task well several times in a row, widen what it may do on that task. [Build trust over time(opens in new tab)](https://claude.com/blog/building-effective-human-agent-teams) ↗
9. **Teach the agent your way of working.**
   When you explain the same thing twice, turn it into written instructions for the agent. [Tutorial: teach Claude your way of working using skills(opens in new tab)](https://academy.claude.com/tutorials/teach-claude-your-way-of-working-using-skills) ↗
10. **Use a weekly retro to improve your process.**
    Have agents and humans share what's working and what requires adjustment at the end of every week. Ask the agents on your team to save these so other agents can use them later.
